WEATHERGPT — USER PERSONAS

Purpose

This document defines the primary users of WeatherGPT.

The product must be designed around real user needs rather than adding features without a clear purpose.

⸻

Persona 1 — General Citizen

Profile

A normal user who wants quick and understandable information about current and upcoming weather.

Problems

* Weather information can be spread across multiple sources.
* Forecast terminology can be difficult to understand.
* Users may not know whether a weather condition is actually important.
* Users want answers to specific questions rather than reading large weather reports.

Needs

* Current weather
* Forecast
* Rain probability
* Severe weather alerts
* Simple explanations
* Personalized recommendations

Example Questions

“Will it rain today?”

“Should I carry an umbrella?”

“Is there any severe weather near me?”

“What will the weather be tonight?”

⸻

Persona 2 — Student

Profile

A student who travels between home, college and other locations.

Problems

* Rain can affect commuting.
* Thunderstorms can create outdoor safety concerns.
* Students often check weather shortly before leaving.

Needs

* College location
* Home location
* Commute weather
* Rain alerts
* Thunderstorm alerts
* Outdoor activity recommendations

Example Question

“Will it rain when I leave college at 5 PM?”

⸻

Persona 3 — Farmer

Profile

A farmer whose agricultural activities depend heavily on weather conditions.

Problems

* Rain can affect irrigation.
* Rain can affect pesticide and fertilizer application.
* Extreme heat can affect crops.
* Strong winds and storms can cause damage.

Needs

* Rain forecast
* Temperature
* Humidity
* Wind
* Agricultural weather advisories
* Extreme weather warnings

Example Question

“Should I irrigate my crop tomorrow?”

Important:

Agricultural recommendations must be presented as weather-informed guidance, not as guaranteed agronomic outcomes.

⸻

Persona 4 — Traveller

Profile

A person planning local or long-distance travel.

Problems

* Weather can change during travel.
* Heavy rainfall can affect roads.
* Fog can reduce visibility.
* Thunderstorms and strong winds can create travel risks.

Needs

* Destination forecast
* Departure weather
* Route weather
* Weather risks
* Travel recommendations

Example Question

“Is tomorrow morning a good time to travel?”

⸻

Persona 5 — Disaster Management Stakeholder

Profile

A person who needs a consolidated view of weather hazards affecting an area.

Problems

* Weather information can come from multiple systems.
* Important warnings need to be identified quickly.
* Large geographical areas need monitoring.

Needs

* Hazard map
* Risk visualization
* Weather alerts
* Multiple locations
* Historical information
* Situation summaries

Example Question

“Which monitored areas currently have high rainfall risk?”

⸻

Persona 6 — Researcher / Analyst

Profile

A user interested in historical weather and climate information.

Problems

* Historical datasets can be difficult to explore.
* Raw weather data can be difficult for non-specialists to interpret.
* Users need comparisons and trends.

Needs

* Historical weather
* Temperature trends
* Rainfall trends
* Anomaly analysis
* Data visualization
* Natural-language querying

Example Questions

“How has the average temperature changed over the last decade?”

“Was this year’s rainfall unusually high?”

⸻

Persona Priority

Primary

1. General Citizen
2. Student
3. Farmer
4. Traveller

Secondary

5. Disaster Management Stakeholder
6. Researcher / Analyst

⸻

Design Principle

The interface must not force every user into the same experience.

WeatherGPT should adapt the information presented according to user context.

For example:

A farmer should see agricultural weather information.

A traveller should see travel-related risks.

A student should see commute and outdoor safety information.

A general citizen should receive simple weather information.

A disaster-management stakeholder should see broader hazard intelligence.

⸻

Personalization Principle

Personalization must change the presentation and recommendations, not the underlying verified weather facts.

The same weather observation must remain the same for every user.

Only the interpretation and relevant actions may change based on the user’s selected context.

⸻

Safety Principle

WeatherGPT must never make users believe that an AI-generated recommendation is an official emergency instruction.

Official warnings must be clearly identified and attributed to their source.

AI-generated recommendations must be clearly distinguished from official warnings.
